🔍 Project Overview

This award-winning project was designed for the 35th WERC Environmental Design Contest. Our team developed a wind-powered, sustainable water filtration system aimed at accelerating ecological recovery after wildfires. The system operates off-grid, using natural filtration media and sensor-based monitoring for real-time feedback.

It earned the Terry McManus Outstanding Student Award for its innovation, environmental impact, and community relevance. The project combined field research, CAD/FEA design, electronics, and hands-on prototyping to deliver a scalable solution for burned soils and contaminated water sources.

🛠️ System Design

The system was structured around three key pillars:

  • Wind-Powered Pumping: A vertical-axis wind turbine drives water through the system without external electricity.
  • Multi-Layer Natural Filtration: The design uses gravel, sand, lava rock, activated coconut charcoal, and cotton layers to purify water.
  • Real-Time Monitoring: Integrated sensors (AHT20 for humidity/temperature, soil moisture, and turbidity) collect live data via an ESP8266 microcontroller and Arduino.

CAD drawings and a physical prototype were fabricated using 3D printing and laser cutting technologies.
